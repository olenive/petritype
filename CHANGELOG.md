# Changelog

## Unreleased

- `PROTOCOL.md` says what a connection's filter is, with nothing on the wire changed and the protocol still version 2: a view a client chooses for itself, to be sent less, which any client may set, change or clear, so a host that must keep some tokens from some clients enforces that itself, in front of the net process (§9.2, §13.4). `distinct` reads every token whatever the filter by design (§13.3), and both runners send the `nested` notifications of a net that a hidden token carries whole, since each inner net has its own filter (§13.4).

## 0.4.1, 2026-11-22

The [`v0.4.1` tag](https://github.com/olenive/petritype/tree/v0.4.1). It carries the protocol text as corrected after a host's review and the fifteen shared transcripts, for a host that replays them from a source checkout.

- `PROTOCOL.md` is corrected after a host's review, with nothing on the wire changed and the protocol still version 2. It says what each runner writes for a firing (the Rust runner reports an outer body with a mutate edge, and every firing inside an inner net, in one piece as `fired`) and what each answers while a body runs; §9 gives a relay its rules, for params in a query string, names in a URL, the net whose version an answer is held for, the envelope's version and a host that speaks several versions; §10 lists every difference of version 2 from version 1; and §5.1, §11 and §12 close smaller gaps, among them a subnet inside a token's net, empty groups, and which shared transcripts run over pipes.
- `PROTOCOL.md` says that the Rust runner reports the real `duration_ms` of a body it runs inline, an outer body with a mutate edge and every firing inside an inner net, from its version 0.2.0; earlier Rust builds wrote 0.0 (§6.3, §12.4, §12.7).
- `PROTOCOL.md` says five things the new transcripts showed it left unsaid: a `load` and a `reset` start with an empty log, `distinct` reads every token whatever filter the connection has, which results about an inner net carry `path` and `nested_id`, a nested net's `count` leaves out the tokens of its subnets, and a failed firing counts in no progress field.
- A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/12_failures.json`, over the conformance net `workshop` (`tests/protocol_nets/workshop.py`, PROTOCOL.md §11): failed firings that lose their token and that give it back, as `failed` with `restored`, the state after each and the log entries, each of the two also for a firing that took its token from a port place; it loads the net twice, without and with its load parameter `restore`. A shared transcript masks a failure's `error` text, which each runtime words its own way, and the recorder masks it; it is replayed over pipes too. Every other transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/13_ports_from_outside.json`, over the conformance net `cellar` (`tests/protocol_nets/cellar.py`, PROTOCOL.md §11): a subnet's port seen from the net around it, its tokens read while the port holds some, a token there changed by that net and told as `mutated`, `removed` and `hidden`, a `query` filter and a `distinct` naming the port by its pair, a `set_filter` addressed to the subnet, and `get_place_tokens` and `inject` by the pair beside `get_token` through the subnet. The recorder names a token that an `inject`'s answer is the first to show, one the filter hid, after the token sent. It is replayed over pipes too, and every other transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/14_subnet_in_a_token.json`, over the conformance net `harbour` (`tests/protocol_nets/harbour.py`, PROTOCOL.md §11): a subnet inside a net that a token carries, its path through the token's step and its nested id, read and stepped there, its path changing as the token moves while its nested id stays, and its end when the token is consumed, after which both nested ids answer `unknown_net`. It is replayed over pipes too, and every other transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/15_odd_names.json`, over the conformance net `odd-names` (`tests/protocol_nets/odd_names.py`, PROTOCOL.md §11): places, transitions, a port, a subnet place and a template whose names hold a slash, a hash, a space and a double quote, addressed by path and carried in requests, results, notifications, progress and the log, and a path step that names a near miss answered `unknown_net`. It is replayed over pipes too, and every other transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- The protocol runner's `heartbeat` names the inner net a body runs in: while a body in a token's net or a subnet runs, at any depth, the heartbeat carries that net's `path` and `nested_id`, the two fields a `step` result carries for a firing there, and leaves both out for a body of the loaded net and when no body runs (PROTOCOL.md §7.2). Until now it named such a body without its net. The two fields are optional, so the protocol stays version 2, and every shared transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- `PROTOCOL.md` says what the `heartbeat` names in both runners, the Python one from 0.4.1 and the Rust one from 0.2.1, and what a host can conclude from it: a heartbeat that names a body means the process is busy in it, however long a read has waited, and two kinds of firing are named by none (§6.3, §7.2). It says how a net is stopped during a body the Rust runner runs inline: `stop` and `shutdown` are read when that body lands, from Rust 0.2.1 a `stop` is answered with nothing more fired first, and a host that must stop such a net sooner ends the process (§3, §6, §6.3).

## 0.4.0, 2026-11-22

The [`v0.4.0` tag](https://github.com/olenive/petritype/tree/v0.4.0).

- The protocol runner serves groups (PROTOCOL.md §5.1): a net's topology lists its groups as `groups`, in their order, and the `group` of each place, subnet place and transition in one, each left out when absent, for the loaded net and every inner net, each its own; a port listed in a subnet place's `ports` carries no `group`, its group being the subnet's. Nothing else on the wire changes, and the protocol stays version 2. A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/11_groups.json`, over the conformance net `groups`; every other transcript re-records to the same bytes.
- Groups: `group=` on `ListPlaceNode`, `SubnetPlaceNode` and `FunctionTransitionNode` names the group the node is in, a label on parts of one net that only drawings read. `ExecutableGraph.groups` lists a net's groups in the order of their first mention, the places read before the transitions. A node is in one group or none, a name may hold any character, and a subnet or a net a token carries has groups of its own. Groups change nothing about how a net runs: it fires, steps, numbers its tokens, records its events and saves exactly as it would with none, and a saved net never mentions them; a copy or a template carries them. The Graphviz drawing frames each group as a dashed cluster titled with its name, in order (`RustworkxToGraphviz.dot_source` and `draw`, which `SimpleGraphvizVisualization` now draws through); a net with no groups draws the source it drew before. The example `examples/groups/hike.py`, with its notebook.
- The protocol runner serves subnet places (PROTOCOL.md §12.7). A subnet is one more inner net, addressed by a path whose step is the subnet place alone, `[{"place": "Level 1"}]`, or by its `nested_id`, known from `load` or `reset` with its own version, topology, progress, log view and `fireable` baseline. The net around it lists a subnet place with its template as `type_name`, its `ports` and its `stepping`, names a port with `port` beside `place` in its edges, `added`, `removed`, `mutated`, `hidden`, `get_place_tokens`, `inject` and `query`'s matches, and as `{"place", "port"}` in `consumed`, `produced` and the log; its marking gives a subnet place's entry as `{subnet, ports}`, and in the subnet's own topology a port place carries `port: true`. A change of a port place raises the versions of both nets that see it, and the notification carries the other's as `subnet_marking_versions` or `outer_marking_version`; both nets' `fireable` sets are compared after it. A read of a subnet waits while a firing of the net around it on one of its ports is in flight, and a read of the net around a subnet while a firing of the subnet on its port is in flight is answered from the port as that firing left it. `Filter.keeps` takes the key a net names a place by, so that a filter restricted to a `Port` matches it, a keyed edge on a port included. The levels example is served as `levels-world`. A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/09_subnet_places.json`, over the conformance net `levels`; the harness folds the versions across a port and the recorder masks them.
- Subnet places: `SubnetPlaceNode(name, graph, stepping="inner_first")` holds a net of its own, its subnet, for the whole life of the net around it, which reaches it only through the places the subnet marks `ListPlaceNode(..., port=True)`. An edge names a port by the pair `Port("Level 1", "Lobby")` where it would name a place, and every input edge kind, every way of choosing an output, guards, keyed edges, `Query` and `Extend`/`SetTokens` then use it as a place of the outer net. A port keeps one list of tokens and one set of ids, the subnet's, whichever net looks. A change is told once, by the net through which it was made: the outer net's events name a port by its pair (`Fired`, `Failed`, `Added`, `Removed` and `Mutated` take a `PlaceKey`), and the subnet's are lifted as `Nested` under `PathStep("Level 1")`, a step with no token. A subnet steps as one net at its place's position under its place's rule, takes no turn while an outer firing on one of its ports is in flight, and is read with `graph.subnet(name)` and stepped or changed by the program inside `NestedNets.subnet_scope`, which raises `SubnetHeldError` meanwhile. `NetJson` saves a subnet built through a template inside its place's entry, a list of one saved form, and refuses one built without, naming the place. The Graphviz drawing shows a subnet place as one rounded box with a cell per port, its name and count, an edge ending at its port's cell, and a port with a second ring in the subnet's own drawing. The build refuses a port the subnet does not have, an edge whose type does not fit its port, an edge on a subnet place, a `Port` beside a place that is not a subnet place, a subnet place marked as a port and one net in two subnet places. `PathStep`'s `token` and `net` default to `None` for a subnet step, and an unknown place in an edge is now a `ValueError` naming it rather than a `KeyError`. The example `examples/subnet_places/levels.py`, with its notebook.
- The protocol runner speaks version 2 of the net protocol (PROTOCOL.md §10). An inner net's `path` is a list of steps from the loaded net down, `{"place", "token", "net"}` for a net carried by a token, in requests, results, a token's `nets`, the `nested` notification, the `step` result and the log, so a place or a net may be named with any character and still be addressed. A path of any other shape is `invalid_params`, and one that names no live net is `unknown_net`, naming the step that failed by its index from 0. A net's progress counts only the firings of its own transitions: the loaded net's `fired_counts` no longer holds a joined name for each firing inside its tokens. The shared transcripts are re-recorded for version 2, and the transcript recorder keeps the names and masks of a transcript it re-records.
- A stepping rule per place that holds nets, `ListPlaceNode(..., nested=True, stepping=...)`: `"inner_first"`, the default, fires the place's nets until they are at rest before the net around them; `"take_turns"` has them fire in turn with it; `"outer_first"` lets them fire only when it and its turn-takers have nothing to fire; `"passive"` leaves them to a body or the program. One deep step tries the inner-first nets, then the ring of the net and its take-turns nets from after the one that fired last, then the outer-first nets, and a net's turn is a deep step of its own, so every level follows the rules of its own places (PROTOCOL.md §12.3). The sequential and concurrent runtimes and the protocol runner step that way, and the runner's topology carries each such place's `stepping`. **Removed:** `construct_graph(deep_policy=...)`, `ExecutableGraph.deep_policy` and `ListPlaceNode(..., passive=True)`; a net that relied on the old default states `stepping="outer_first"` on its places, as the transit example does. A path inside the library is a tuple of `PathStep(place, token, net)` values, and a firing inside an inner net is an `InnerFiring(path, transition)`, which `fire_one_transition` returns and `fired_counts`, `last_fired` and `RunSummary.fired` count it under, where they held a name joined with `/`. The concurrent runtime now fires an inner net beside outer bodies in flight, except the nets of a token such a body holds, and the sequential fuse counts the inner nets a deep step would enter. A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/10_stepping_rules.json`, over the conformance net `stepping-rules`.

## 0.3.1, 2026-10-17

The [`v0.3.1` tag](https://github.com/olenive/petritype/tree/v0.3.1).

- Sequential runs notify observers as a transition goes in flight, before its body runs, as concurrent runs already did, so a renderer on the event loop can show it working; an observer that raises at that notification fails the firing as a raising body does, with a `LaunchNotificationError` as the `TransitionFailedError`'s cause.

## 0.3.0, 2026-10-17

The [`v0.3.0` tag](https://github.com/olenive/petritype/tree/v0.3.0).

- Nets as tokens: a `nested=True` place holds tokens that carry Petri nets of their own, which the outer net runs, logs and reaches inside, and a net saves and restores as JSON through a registered template (PROTOCOL.md §12).
- Token keys, queries and filters: places derive keys from their tokens, `Filter` and `Query` select by them (`Query.distinct` lists a key's values), `TakeWhereEdge` and `MutateWhereEdge` act on matching tokens only, and the protocol runner answers `query`, `distinct` and `set_filter` (PROTOCOL.md §13).
- `DrainEdge` and `SurveyEdge`, input edges that accept an empty place: a drain takes every token and a survey copies every token, zero or more either way.
- Marimo notebooks for the transit journey, the wildlife filter, the shop day, the traffic light junction and the rooms state machine.
- The traffic light (`examples/edges_on_empty_places/traffic_light.py`), a drain beside a plain read, with its shared transcript `tests/protocol/08_traffic_light.json`.
- Net-declared controls and place styles, `@petri_net(controls=..., place_styles=...)`, served as `topology.controls` and `places[].style`; the protocol's `press` runs a control, and the `Fire` command fires a named transition if it is fireable.
- A subclass of a JSON scalar, such as `class Colour(str)`, is serializable with its scalar's JSON form.
