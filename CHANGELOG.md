# Changelog

## 0.6.0, unreleased

A minor version, since several changes can break a working program: each entry marked as such says what the program changes. The protocol stays version 2. The shared transcripts change only where the rule on closed nets changes them, as the first entry and the one on the nets inside a closed net say.

- **A change that can break a program: every net is closed unless it declares itself open.** In 0.5.0 only a net that marked a port was closed. Now every net is, unless its definition says `construct_graph(..., open=True)`: the net a program loads or a `RunContext` drives as much as a net a token carries or a subnet. From outside it, tokens are put, taken and replaced only at its ports, so a net that marks no port takes none from outside at all. Outside are the program and its drivers, with `NestedNets.put` and `NestedNets.take` and the inbox commands `Extend` and `SetTokens`, the protocol runner's `inject`, and a body of the net around it. A refused write raises `ClosedNetError`; a body that makes one fails its firing with it as the cause; the runner answers the `inject` with `closed_net`; and an inbox command raises it out of the run in every `RunContext`, strict or not: a place that is not a port is a matter of the net's definition, so the command is not dropped and logged as one that will not decode is. The tokens a net's definition starts it with, its own firings and the commands of its own controls (`apply_control`, the runner's `press`) are inside and unaffected. A program written for 0.5.0 makes each refused write land as the message says: it starts the net with the token in its definition, as a seed or a template's token; it marks the place `port=True` where the net takes input from outside, and names that port in the `reaches` of the edge whose body writes it; or, for a net meant to be written anywhere from outside, it declares the net open. `CLOSED_WITHOUT_PORTS` and `ClosedNets.under_the_rule` are gone: a net's definition alone says whether it is closed. The transit example's cabins mark as ports the door and the places `Arrive` writes, and each edge of the city that hands a body a vehicle names the ports its body reaches. The shared transcripts change with it: their nets mark ports where they take input from outside, which their topologies show as `port`, an `inject` by a port pair into a closed net is answered `closed_net` or sent by path to the subnet instead, and the transit cabins in `05_nested_nets.json` have no `stopped`.
- **A change that can break a program: a body sets the knobs of no net it holds.** In 0.5.0 a body could set the parameters, switches and keywords of a net it held that marked no port, and fire it by name; now every net a body holds, open or closed, refuses them with `HeldByBodyError`, which fails the firing: `NetParams.set` and `NestedNets.set_param`, the runtime's `SetNetParam`, `SetParam`, `Enable`, `Disable` and `Fire`, a selector of the body's own, and a change made directly to `params`, `disabled` or a transition's `kwargs`, found when the body returns. A program gives such a setting to the program or the runner, or to a transition of the held net's own that makes the change from a token the body puts at a port. The transit example's cabins lose their `stopped` parameter, which `Depart` and `Arrive` set and nothing read: a cabin is under way when every door is closed, and `Arrive` opens the doors at its ports. A body is refused the knobs of a net inside one it holds as well. While a body holds a net the program is refused its knobs and a load into it too, as it was the knobs of a net that marked a port, and is told to wait for the landing; such a refusal is the program's alone, where in 0.5.0 it also failed the firing of the body that held the net.
- **A change that can break a program: a `bool` is not an `int`.** `True` and `False` no longer pass as an `int` (`CompareTypes.between_value_and_type`), nor inside `list[int]`, `int | str` or a `type` alias of `int`: a place built with one raises `TypeError`, an `Extend` or `SetTokens` of one is refused, and a body that returns one there fails its firing. Until now they passed, since `bool` is a subclass of `int` in Python. A place that holds flags is typed `bool` or `int | bool`, or the program turns them into integers.
- **A change that can break a program: the node and edge classes refuse an argument they would have dropped.** `ListPlaceNode`, `SubnetPlaceNode`, `FunctionTransitionNode` and every edge class (`PositionalArgsBaseModel`) raise `TypeError` for more positional arguments than they take by position, or for an argument given both by position and by keyword; until now the extra positional arguments were dropped silently and the keyword won. A program gives each argument once.
- **A change that can break a program: a `transition_selector` chooses among the transitions it is offered.** One that returns any other transition raises `ValueError`, in sequential and in concurrent mode. Until now the engine fired what it returned, past a failing guard if its tokens were there, and raised `IndexError` if they were not. A selector returns one of the transitions it is handed, or `None` to fire none.
- **A change that can break a program: lookups by name are kept.** `ExecutableGraph.place_named`, `ExecutableGraph.transition_named` and the maps a firing runs on (`ExecutableGraphOperations.maps`) are built once and kept until one of the net's lists of nodes or edges changes length, where each call built them afresh. A program that edits a built net's structure where it lies, replacing a place, a transition or an edge, or renaming one, then calls the new `ExecutableGraph.structure_changed()` on that net and on each net around it with edges on its ports; until it does, the lookups answer with the nodes that were there before.
- **A change that can break a program: `petritype[viz]` no longer installs matplotlib.** It is in the `examples` extra, and `petritype.plotting.simple_graphviz` no longer needs it, so `viz` installs `rustworkx` and `pillow`. A program that uses matplotlib installs it, or `petritype[examples]`.
- **A change that can break a program: the rule on closed nets sees an edit inside a held net's parameters and keywords.** When a body returns, the check of each net it held compares the plain containers in its `params` and in each transition's `kwargs`, to any depth: exactly a `dict`, `list`, `tuple`, `set` or `frozenset`. A body that changed one in place, appending to a list in `params` say, fails its firing with `HeldByBodyError`, where 0.5.0 let it pass unseen; such a change is left to the program, a control or a transition of the held net. Any other object there, a subclass of those included, is kept by reference, so a change inside it goes unseen, as inside a token, and a body that changes nothing never fails, whatever the values are.
- **A change that can break a program: a body writes only the ports its edge reaches.** An input edge's `reaches`, a label for readers and drawings in 0.5.0, is enforced, so that the code and the drawing cannot disagree: a body that puts tokens in, or takes them from, a port of a closed net it holds, through `NestedNets.put` or `NestedNets.take` or the runtime's `Extend` or `SetTokens`, fails its firing with `ClosedNetError`, its new `undeclared` true, unless the edge that handed it the net names the port. Until now such a write landed at any port. A program names each port its body writes in that edge's `reaches`, `MutateEdge("Field", "Hand Over", "unit", reaches=("Orders In",))`, as the message says. A body writes no port of a closed net inside a net it holds either, a net a token there carries or a subnet, at any depth and by any route, the net itself, `NestedNets.subnet_scope` or a port pair through an open net it holds, at a port or at a place of the net's own: that fails its firing with `ClosedNetError`, its new `deeper` true, since an edge names ports of the net it hands over and nothing drawn could show such a reach, and the body puts the token at a port of the net it holds for a transition of that net to carry further in. Nor may a body load a marking or a saved form (`import_marking`, `NetJson.load_into`) into a net it holds, open or closed, or into a net inside one, which would replace the tokens of every place: `HeldByBodyError`. Reading is not restricted, a port named and not used is no error, and the program, the runner, a body writing a net it built and a net declared open are as before.
- **A change that can break a program: a closed net shields the nets inside it.** Until now the program and the runner wrote a port of any net by addressing it, a subnet of a closed net or a net one of its tokens carries included: through `NestedNets.subnet_scope(world, "Level 1")` or `world.subnet("Level 1")`, through the token's net itself, by a `RunContext` on it, or by an `inject` by `path` or `nested_id`. So a token could skip the route the closed net draws, a hero put straight into level 1's lobby past the world's `Enter`. Now a write by the program, its drivers or the runner at a net inside another, at any depth, is refused with `ClosedNetError`, at its ports too, unless every net around it is declared open; the error's new `around` names the nearest closed net around, its message says to come in through that net's ports or to declare it open, and the runner answers the `inject` with `closed_net`. The inner net's own rule applies on top, so through open nets the write lands at its ports, and anywhere only if it is open too. A body that holds a net is held to its edge's `reaches` there, as before; at any other net it is outside as the program is, so it is refused at a net no edge handed it that is inside a closed net (one it reached through a closure, or a net inside a closed net it built itself), and at every net inside a closed net it holds, where until now an open one let its write land. The engine's own moves are as before, a firing's outputs into a subnet's port and a deep step's inner firings. A net loaded by `pickle` is a new net with its own `net_id`, as a deep copy is, and the nets inside it are inside it. A program written for 0.5.0 makes a refused write land as the message says: it gives the closed net around a port and a transition that carries the token in, `Door -Enter-> Port("Level 1", "Lobby")`, which the drawing then shows, or a body whose edge names the inner net's port in its `reaches`; or, for nets meant to be written anywhere from outside, it declares the net around open, and every net around that, which opens their own places too. A net is inside the net whose place the engine last saw it come to rest in while that place still holds it, so a net the program takes out of its place is written as before, one a firing moves is judged where it lands, and one a body holds counts as inside until the firing lands. A net that code appends to a place's list directly is shielded once an engine entry point next gives that place's tokens their ids. Loading a marking or a saved form into a net inside a closed net (`import_marking`, `NetJson.load_into`) is refused the same way, the error's `place` being `None`, since it writes every place of the net and would skip more than a put does; a program loads the net around as a whole, which loads the nets inside it, or declares the net around open. The knobs of a net inside a closed net stay with the program, as on every net. The conformance nets `workshop`, `post-office` and the `house` around the `cellar` are declared open, so that the shared transcripts' requests into the nets inside them land; `transit` and `levels` stay closed.
- **A change that can break a program: a question changes no net.** A transition's `guard` and `priority`, a place's key functions, a `transition_selector` and an `output_distribution_function` are refused every change to every net by the library's functions, with the new `ReadOnlyError` (a `RuntimeError`): `NestedNets.put` and `NestedNets.take`, `NetParams.set` and `NestedNets.set_param`, `NetJson.import_marking` and `NetJson.load_into`, and a control of the net, pressed or applied (`apply_control`, a `by_control` command); on their own net or any other, open or closed, at a port or not. Until now each wrote as the program does, so a guard could put a token at any port: a change no drawing shows, made between the library planning a firing and making it. Whatever asked fails with the error, also when the function caught it: a sequential step, a concurrent run and a query raise it, and a firing whose output distribution function met it fails. The protocol runner, where a guard that raises cannot let its transition fire, says the refusal once on stderr. A function that changed a net gives the change to a transition's body, or leaves it to the program between firings. Reading is not restricted. Nothing in the library's examples or conformance nets wrote so. A two-key lookup costs about 150 ns more per token for it.
- **A change that can break a program: concurrent mode keeps firings off each other's tokens and follows the selector.** Under `ExecutionMode.CONCURRENT`, as the next entries say, two bodies no longer work on one token at once, a consumer waits for a token a body in flight reads or mutates instead of taking it, and priorities, `tie_break` and a custom `transition_selector` decide what launches. A net that relied on the old behaviour keeps slow reads and mutations off the places other transitions consume from, and its custom selector is asked again after each launch, with the transitions that can still launch, and returns `None` to launch no more.
- **A change that can break a program: a body leaves the events, the tie-break cursor and the controls of a net it holds to the program.** A body that holds a net, or a net that one is inside, is refused with `HeldByBodyError`, which fails its firing also when the body caught it, as a knob's refusal does: `drain_events()` on it, whose events the net around gathers into its own log as the firing lands, where until now they were gone from that log; `default_transition_selector` called on it, or its `forget_cursors()`, each of which moved its tie-break cursor without a firing; and pressing one of its controls, by `apply_control` or by draining a command marked `by_control`, which until now landed as from inside the net, at a port its edge does not reach too (decision D3, as in the Rust library). A step the body makes of the net chooses with the default selector as before, and the program, its drivers, the runner and a viewer keep all three. A body that drives a net it holds with a `RunContext` whose `disabled` switches transitions off is refused in both drivers before anything fires, since the switches choose which transition fires: until now the sequential driver refused it as a selector of the caller's own and the concurrent one let the switches choose. A body leaves such work to the program, or to a transition of the held net's own that acts on a token the body puts at a port.
- **A change that can break a program: a marking is not loaded under a firing in flight.** `import_marking` and `NetJson.load_into`, so `NetJson.restore` too, raise `HeldByBodyError`, its `holder` the new `"in_flight"`, and change nothing while a firing of the net is in flight, or of one of its subnets at any depth, or of the net around it with an edge on one of its ports; until now the load was taken and the landing put the firing's outputs into the marking loaded. A body that loads its own net is refused the same way. A program loads between firings, as the protocol runner's `load` and `reset` do: they wait for the landing, as every request but a read does, and build a new net. `HeldByBodyError` takes `why` and `who`, words it gives in place of its own.
- **A change that can break a program: a NaN priority is an error.** The default selector raises `ValueError` naming the transition whose `priority` returned NaN, so whatever asked fails, a step, a concurrent sweep or the protocol runner's `step`, answered `internal`, as when a priority raises (decision D5). Until now the pick depended on the order of declaration and the tie-break, and `tie_break="round_robin"` raised `IndexError`. Infinity ranks as itself, as before, and the protocol runner leaves `priority` out for both, as it does when a priority raises. A priority function returns a number.
- **A change that can break a program: two output edges from one transition to one place are refused.** `construct_graph` raises `ValueError` naming the transition and the place, a port pair included (decision D6). Until now the net built and its firing failed, or, with `allow_token_copying` on or with return indices, one token landed where the drawing showed two. A transition that puts two tokens in one place returns them as a list on one edge. Several input edges of one transition on one place stay allowed, each taking a token of its own.
- **A change that can break a program: a control of a net a body holds is pressed once the firing lands.** While a body holds a net, the program, its drivers and any other body are refused a press of one of its controls with `HeldByBodyError`, its `holder` `"other"`, as they are its knobs and a load into it: `apply_control` raises it at once, and a command marked `by_control` that is drained meanwhile is refused as a knob's command is, raised under `strict` and dropped and logged otherwise. The refusal is the program's alone, so the body is not failed for it. Until now the press landed, and the body was failed when it returned if the press had changed a knob or a place that is no port; in 0.5.0 a `SetNetParam` given by a control was refused and the rest landed. A press while no body holds the net lands as before, and the protocol runner's `press` waits for the landing, as every request but a read does. A program presses again once the firing has landed.
- **A change that can break a program: no name is empty.** A place, a subnet place, a transition (so a port, which is a place of its subnet), the group of each, a key of a place, a keyword of a transition, a parameter of the net, its template and the argument of an input edge are refused the empty string as their node or net is built, with `ValueError` (pydantic's `ValidationError`); so are a net and a control in `petri_net` (a control-map given as a callable when the protocol runner loads the net, answered `load_failed`), and a keyword in a `SetParam` the runtime applies. Until now only an empty entry of an edge's `reaches` was refused, and an empty template name passed `construct_graph` though `Templates.register` refused it. A name may hold any character, a space included, and at least one. A token that is itself a net still carries it under the name `""`, and a control's `node` of `""` still targets no node.
- **A change that can break a program: a control is checked as it is declared.** `ControlSpec` raises `ValueError` as it is made for a `kind` or an `action` outside the vocabulary, a `node` or a `label` that is no string, a `to_command` that cannot be called, a `config` that is no dict, and, by kind: a toggle's `value` that is no boolean; a slider's `start`, `stop`, `step` or `value` that is no finite number, `start` above `stop`, a `step` not above zero, or a `value` outside the range given; a select with no `options`, an option that is no string, or a `value` that is none of them; a text's `placeholder` or `value` that is no string (`ControlSpec.validate`, as the Rust library plans). Until now it took anything, and a kind or an action outside the vocabulary failed only the protocol runner's load. What the config leaves out still takes the widget's default, and its other keys are the runtime's own; every control in the examples, the notebooks and the tests passes.
- **A change that can break a program: the acyclic check follows a subnet place through.** `ExecutableGraphCheck.assert_acyclic`, which `construct_graph(..., expect_acyclic=True)` calls, goes on from a port of a subnet place to every port the subnet's own token flow leads to, at any depth (`ExecutableGraphCheck.port_paths`, `token_flow`), so a cycle that leaves the net at one port and comes back at another, or one inside the subnet that comes back to the port it left from, raises `ValueError`, its path naming the way through as `(inside subnet place 'S')`. Until now the check stopped at the ports and such a net passed. A cycle wholly inside a subnet, through none of its ports, is the subnet's own, checked on the subnet. No example or test net that asks for the check changes.
- **A change that can break a program: the protocol runner caps a request line.** A request line is 4 MiB at most without its newline, or `PETRITYPE_RUNNER_MAX_LINE_BYTES` bytes when that environment variable holds a positive integer, as `PETRITYPE_RUNNER_HEARTBEAT_MS` sets the heartbeat (PROTOCOL.md §3). A longer line is discarded up to its newline as it is read, never held whole, and answered with `invalid_params` and the `id` `""`, as a line that is not JSON is; the runner serves the lines after it. Until now a line of any length was read whole into memory. A host that sends longer requests raises the cap.
- **A change that can break a program: a concurrent run that ends on an error lets the firings in flight land first, a failed firing's peers included.** In 0.5.0 a concurrent run that ended on an error cancelled every body still in flight, without waiting for it, and what those bodies had consumed was lost, as were the outputs of the bodies that had finished in the same batch as a failing one. Now, on an error, a body that failed, a guard, a priority or a selector that raised in the middle of a sweep, the fuse, a refused command or an observer that raised, the bodies in flight finish, for the net's `cancellation_timeout_seconds` at most, and their outputs are deposited and their events recorded before the error goes on, in `Runner.run`, `Runner.run_to_completion` and `Runner.run_indefinitely` alike, a body on a thread included, as in the Rust library. Bodies that finished together land in the order launched, so of two failures in one batch the one launched first is raised. A peer that fails as it lands fails as any firing does, with `restore_tokens_on_failure` and its `Failed` record, and is told in a note on the error, which stays the first failure; a body still running at the timeout is cancelled and named in a note. The failing firing itself is handled as before. A program that relied on a failure stopping the other bodies sees them finish: their side effects happen, their outputs are in their places and counted in `fired_counts`, and the error comes up to the timeout later, 300 s by default. Such a program sets a shorter `cancellation_timeout_seconds`, or cancels the run itself, which still cancels every body in flight at once, as a stop does. Until 2026-11-28 this version cancelled the bodies in flight on every error, waiting for them to stop.
- In concurrent mode each firing claims the tokens it takes or is handed, and a transition whose claims conflict with those of a firing in flight waits. Read edges share a token, mutate and consuming edges need it alone, a snapshot waits only for a mutator, since it copies at launch, and an edge that takes or reads every token (a drain, a survey, a read-all or a list-mode edge) claims the whole place; firings on different tokens of one place, through keyed edges for instance, still overlap. Claims are checked all at once before a launch and nothing is held while a transition waits, so firings cannot deadlock. Until now two mutators of one token ran at once, and another transition could take a token a body was mutating or reading, since mutate and read edges leave it in its place.
- The trade is head-of-line waiting: a transition waits for the token it would take in sequential mode, the front token or the oldest match of a keyed edge, rather than skip to the next, so a slow reader or mutator of the front token holds back the consumers of every token behind it. With three tokens, a 50 ms reader of the front token and a plain consumer, the consumer now takes nothing until the read ends; in 0.5.0 it took all three during the read.
- Each concurrent launch sweep picks one transition at a time with the net's `transition_selector` or the default one, so `priority`, `tie_break` and a custom selector decide which of two transitions after one token gets it, in `Runner.run` and `Runner.run_indefinitely` alike. Until now concurrent mode launched in declaration order and never asked a custom selector.
- A `Fire` command applied while a concurrent loop runs on its context is held to the same claims: when they conflict it does nothing, as when its transition cannot fire, and otherwise it takes the very tokens it was checked by. It no longer empties `graph.in_flight` of the loop's own firings, which let a second `Fire` fire a transition still in flight again and let `NestedNets.subnet_scope` open a subnet that a firing in flight held still. With no concurrent loop running, a `Fire` fires as before.
- A body handed every token of a place to change, by a mutate edge in list mode, holds that place's own list, so the net of a token that lands there while the body runs is not stepped until the body lands; until now the concurrent loop could fire it under the body's hands.
- Concurrent mode honours `allow_token_copying`: one returned object routed to two places gives each its own copy, where the firing failed as if copying were off.
- When a concurrent run is cancelled or stopped, the async bodies in flight are cancelled and awaited before the run ends, so their `finally` blocks run first, and so are those still running when a run that ends on an error has waited for them to land (the entry on a failed firing's peers, above); a body on a thread cannot be interrupted, as before. The wait is for the net's `cancellation_timeout_seconds` at most, 300 by default and set per net with `construct_graph(..., cancellation_timeout_seconds=N)`, `None` for no limit, so a body that catches `asyncio.CancelledError` and carries on cannot keep the run from ending: a warning that names the bodies still running is logged at three fifths of the timeout, and past it the run lets go of them and names them, in a note on the error it was ending with, or in a new `CancellationTimeoutError` when it was ending with none (a stopped `run_indefinitely`).
- A body may put a new net in a token it was lent through a mutate edge (decision D2), and the new net is told as a new net: it has its own `net_id`, the old one is no longer found in the net (`NestedNets.find`), its events are lifted under the new id, and the protocol runner gives it a new `nested_id` and answers the old one with `unknown_net`. The new net is inside the place as the old one was, so a closed net around shields it; until now nothing noted where it rested, and the program wrote any port of it.
- A refusal by the rule on closed nets fails only the firing whose body made it: a concurrent peer that holds the same net lands as it would have, where it failed too.
- The claims cover only the firings the runner launches and those a `Fire` makes. Python cannot stop a body, a guard, an observer or the program from changing a token or the net directly, nor a body from keeping a token after it returns; the README says so, as it does for closed nets.
- One cost comes with choosing one launch at a time: the selector is asked again after each launch, with every transition that can still launch, so a run of 400 independent transitions takes about 0.02 s and one of 1,600 about 0.17 s, three quarters of it in the selector, where 0.5.0, which launched in declaration order, took about 0.01 s and 0.04 s.
- A firing's tokens are chosen for all its input edges before any moves (`ExecutableGraphOperations.plan_stage_1`, shared by the fireability check, the firing, the concurrent claims and the protocol runner). Several input edges of one transition on one place each get a token of their own, so two takes from a place of one token wait for a second, as does a take beside a snapshot of that place; and a firing that cannot get every token takes none, where it could empty some places and then raise `IndexError`.
- A firing takes all its tokens or none: stage 1 makes every copy it hands over, a snapshot's, a survey's and the token history's, before it removes any token. A copy that raises, from a token whose `__deepcopy__` fails, fails the firing as a raising body does, with `TransitionFailedError` and the copy's error as its cause and a `Failed` record, which the protocol runner tells as `failed`; the tokens it would have taken are told as consumed and restored, with their ids, whatever `restore_tokens_on_failure` says, since none left its place. Until now the consumed tokens were lost, also with `restore_tokens_on_failure`, the error was the copy's own and nothing was recorded. The nets of the tokens it hands a body live are asked for before any token is removed too, so a `__petritype_nets__` that raises anything but `TypeError` there fails the firing the same way (`ClosedNets.nets_lent`), where the tokens taken were lost and the error was the token's own.
- `restore_tokens_on_failure` puts tokens back in their order when two edges of the failed transition took from one place: `[1, 2, 3, 4]`, taken by an `int` and a `list[int]` argument, comes back as it was, where it came back `[2, 3, 4, 1]`.
- A token is checked against more kinds of place type: `set[...]`, `frozenset[...]`, `Mapping[...]`, `Sequence[...]` and the other collection generics check their items, `tuple[X, ...]` takes a tuple of any length, `type[T]` a class, `Annotated[T, ...]` what `T` takes, `Literal[...]` its values with their own types, and a custom generic an instance of its class. Until now `tuple[X, ...]` took no tuple and each of the others raised `TypeError`. `None` passes as a `type` alias of an optional, as `type(None)` and as `object`, where it did not.
- `NestedNets.take` refuses, with `ValueError` and taking nothing, a count that is negative or not an integer, `True` and `False` included; until now `-1` and `True` each took a token.
- `NetJson.load_into` and `NetJson.restore` copy the saved form's `params`, so the net shares no list or dict with the object it was loaded from.
- `Query.distinct` and the protocol runner's `distinct` run only the extractor of the key they are asked for, where they ran every key's.
- `model_dump()` of a net that keeps a place history no longer warns: `input_place_history` and `output_place_history` are typed as what they hold, one list of places per firing.
- `PetritypeError` is a new base class of the seven errors of the engine and the runtime, `CancellationTimeoutError`, `ClosedNetError`, `HeldByBodyError`, `TransitionFailedError`, `LaunchNotificationError`, `SubnetHeldError` and `TooManyFiringsError`, each of which keeps its `ValueError` or `RuntimeError` base; the protocol runner's `ProtocolError` is not one.
- The protocol runner leaves out the `priority` of a transition whose priority is NaN or infinite, as PROTOCOL.md §2 asks of an optional field, where it wrote `"priority": null`. `Frames.encode`, which writes the process's lines, refuses NaN and infinity with `ValueError`, where it wrote `NaN` or `Infinity`, which JSON does not have.
- A line the protocol runner cannot write as JSON costs that line and not the process: an answer becomes an `internal` error to its request, a notification is left out, which a host meets as a gap in the version if it moved one (PROTOCOL.md §4), and either is reported on stderr. Until now the refusal stopped the process, with the loaded net and its marking; no ordinary request was found to reach it, every known source of a NaN being turned away earlier.
- A pydantic model token that holds a NaN or an infinity has no JSON form, as a bare float that is one has none: `get_token` answers `kind: "text"`, a `query` match carries no `json`, and `NetJson.save` raises `ValueError` naming the place and the field. - The protocol runner serves groups (PROTOCOL.md §5.1): a net's topology lists its groups as `groups`, in their order, and the `group` of each place, subnet place and transition in one, each left out when absent, for the loaded net and every inner net, each its own; a port listed in a subnet place's `ports` carries no `group`, its group being the subnet's. A node is in one group or none, a name may hold any character, and a subnet or a net a token carries has groups of its own. Groups change nothing about how a net runs: it fires, steps, numbers its tokens, records its events and saves exactly as it would with none, and a saved net never mentions them; a copy or a template carries them. The Graphviz drawing frames each group as a dashed cluster titled with its name, in order (`RustworkxToGraphviz.dot_source` and `draw`, which `SimpleGraphvizVisualization` now draws through); a net with no groups draws the source it drew before. The example `examples/groups/hike.py`, with its notebook.
- The protocol runner serves subnet places (PROTOCOL.md §12.7). A subnet is one more inner net, addressed by a path whose step is the subnet place alone, `[{"place": "Level 1"}]`, or by its `nested_id`, known from `load` or `reset` with its own version, topology, progress, log view and `fireable` baseline. The net around it lists a subnet place with its template as `type_name`, its `ports` and its `stepping`, names a port with `port` beside `place` in its edges, `added`, `removed`, `mutated`, `hidden`, `get_place_tokens`, `inject` and `query`'s matches, and as `{"place", "port"}` in `consumed`, `produced` and the log; its marking gives a subnet place's entry as `{subnet, ports}`, and in the subnet's own topology a port place carries `port: true`. A change of a port place raises the versions of both nets that see it, and the notification carries the other's as `subnet_marking_versions` or `outer_marking_version`; both nets' `fireable` sets are compared after it. A read of a subnet waits while a firing of the net around it on one of its ports is in flight, and a read of the net around a subnet while a firing of the subnet on its port is in flight is answered from the port as that firing left it. `Filter.keeps` takes the key a net names a place by, so that a filter restricted to a `Port` matches it, a keyed edge on a port included. The levels example is served as `levels-world`. A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/09_subnet_places.json`, over the conformance net `levels`; the harness folds the versions across a port and the recorder masks them.
- Subnet places: `SubnetPlaceNode(name, graph, stepping="inner_first")` holds a net of its own, its subnet, for the whole life of the net around it, which reaches it only through the places the subnet marks `ListPlaceNode(..., port=True)`. An edge names a port by the pair `Port("Level 1", "Lobby")` where it would name a place, and every input edge kind, every way of choosing an output, guards, keyed edges, `Query` and `Extend`/`SetTokens` then use it as a place of the outer net. A port keeps one list of tokens and one set of ids, the subnet's, whichever net looks. A change is told once, by the net through which it was made: the outer net's events name a port by its pair (`Fired`, `Failed`, `Added`, `Removed` and `Mutated` take a `PlaceKey`), and the subnet's are lifted as `Nested` under `PathStep("Level 1")`, a step with no token. A subnet steps as one net at its place's position under its place's rule, takes no turn while an outer firing on one of its ports is in flight, and is read with `graph.subnet(name)` and stepped or changed by the program inside `NestedNets.subnet_scope`, which raises `SubnetHeldError` meanwhile. `NetJson` saves a subnet built through a template inside its place's entry, a list of one saved form, and refuses one built without, naming the place. The Graphviz drawing shows a subnet place as one rounded box with a cell per port, its name and count, an edge ending at its port's cell, and a port with a second ring in the subnet's own drawing. The build refuses a port the subnet does not have, an edge whose type does not fit its port, an edge on a subnet place, a `Port` beside a place that is not a subnet place, a subnet place marked as a port and one net in two subnet places. `PathStep`'s `token` and `net` default to `None` for a subnet step, and an unknown place in an edge is now a `ValueError` naming it rather than a `KeyError`. The example `examples/subnet_places/levels.py`, with its notebook.
- The protocol runner speaks version 2 of the net protocol (PROTOCOL.md §10). An inner net's `path` is a list of steps from the loaded net down, `{"place", "token", "net"}` for a net carried by a token, in requests, results, a token's `nets`, the `nested` notification, the `step` result and the log, so a place or a net may be named with any character and still be addressed. A path of any other shape is `invalid_params`, and one that names no live net is `unknown_net`, naming the step that failed by its index from 0. A net's progress counts only the firings of its own transitions: the loaded net's `fired_counts` no longer holds a joined name for each firing inside its tokens. The shared transcripts are re-recorded for version 2, and the transcript recorder keeps the names and masks of a transcript it re-records.
- A stepping rule per place that holds nets, `ListPlaceNode(..., nested=True, stepping=...)`: `"inner_first"`, the default, fires the place's nets until they are at rest before the net around them; `"take_turns"` has them fire in turn with it; `"outer_first"` lets them fire only when it and its turn-takers have nothing to fire; `"passive"` leaves them to a body or the program. One deep step tries the inner-first nets, then the ring of the net and its take-turns nets from after the one that fired last, then the outer-first nets, and a net's turn is a deep step of its own, so every level follows the rules of its own places (PROTOCOL.md §12.3). The sequential and concurrent runtimes and the protocol runner step that way, and the runner's topology carries each such place's `stepping`. **Removed:** `construct_graph(deep_policy=...)`, `ExecutableGraph.deep_policy` and `ListPlaceNode(..., passive=True)`; a net that relied on the old default states `stepping="outer_first"` on its places, as the transit example does. A path inside the library is a tuple of `PathStep(place, token, net)` values, and a firing inside an inner net is an `InnerFiring(path, transition)`, which `fire_one_transition` returns and `fired_counts`, `last_fired` and `RunSummary.fired` count it under, where they held a name joined with `/`. The concurrent runtime now fires an inner net beside outer bodies in flight, except the nets of a token such a body holds, and the sequential fuse counts the inner nets a deep step would enter. A new shared transcript, `tests/protocol/10_stepping_rules.json`, over the conformance net `stepping-rules`.
